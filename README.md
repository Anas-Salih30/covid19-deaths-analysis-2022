# covid19-deaths-analysis-2022
COVID-19 reported deaths analysis in Sudan, Egypt, Kenya, and South Africa (2022)

## Project Overview

This project analyzes reported COVID-19 deaths in Sudan, Egypt, Kenya, and South Africa during 2022.

## Dataset

The dataset contains daily records of weekly reported COVID-19 deaths per million people for four countries during 2022.

## Countries

- Sudan
- Egypt
- Kenya
- South Africa

## Tools

- Microsoft Excel
- GitHub

## Preliminary Analysis

The initial analysis compares the average reported weekly COVID-19 deaths per million people across the four countries and examines monthly trends during 2022.

## Preliminary Findings

- South Africa recorded the highest annual average of weekly reported deaths per million people (3.60).
- Kenya recorded the lowest annual average (0.11).
- Sudan recorded its highest monthly average in March (4.58).
- Egypt recorded its highest monthly average in February (3.17).

## Data Quality Checks

- Each country has 365 daily records.
- The dataset covers 01 January 2022 to 31 December 2022 for all four countries.
- No duplicate country-date records were identified.
- The dataset contains 1,460 records in total.

## Analysis and Key Findings

### Annual Country Comparison

- South Africa recorded the highest annual average of reported weekly COVID-19 deaths per million people (3.60).
- Kenya recorded the lowest annual average (0.11).
- Sudan recorded an annual average of 0.65, while Egypt recorded 0.54.

### Monthly Trends

- South Africa recorded its highest monthly average in February (17.59).
- Egypt recorded its highest monthly average in February (3.17).
- Sudan recorded its highest monthly average in March (4.58).
- Kenya recorded its highest monthly average in January (0.85).

### Interpretation

The data show differences in reported COVID-19 death rates across the four countries during 2022. South Africa recorded the highest annual average, while Kenya recorded the lowest. Monthly trends also varied between countries, with the highest monthly averages occurring in different months. These findings describe the reported data and do not necessarily explain the causes of the differences.
